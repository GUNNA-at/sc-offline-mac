# Design System: sc-offline-mac

**Two surfaces, two systems.** This project has two surfaces, and they do not share a visual system.

- **The README** on github.com follows "The Pilot's Operating Handbook". Everything in this file that is not under a heading beginning with "Launcher" describes the README and binds the README only. That includes its rules that no component is interactive, that the page is static, that there are no rounded containers, shadows or gradients, that green, yellow and red are never used for status, its Helvetica and Courier lettering, its one lettering size, and its type ramp.
- **The launcher**, the macOS app in `launcher/` whose interface is `launcher/ui/index.html`, `app.css` and `app.js`, follows "The Launcher, Played Straight". It is described under the heading "Launcher" at the end of each section, and its tokens carry the prefix `launcher-`. It has its own colours, type ramp, radii, state colours, interactive components and motion rules.

Neither system borrows from the other. A token without the prefix is never used in the app, and a `launcher-` token is never used in a figure. What the two share is the project's wording: ranges are written with the word "to", an en dash stands for a value that was not measured, and no number is given for a Mac that was not measured.

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Pilot's Operating Handbook"**

The README is an operating handbook written the way a real one is: for a type, and flight-tested on one airframe. The type is every Mac with Apple silicon flying Star Citizen offline; the airframe is the one machine it has run on, a MacBook Pro 16-inch with an M4 Pro and 24 GB. The line under the title names the type, the data plate names the airframe (TESTED ON), and every figure with numbers says whose numbers they are. It documents the machine the way pilots document an aircraft, with a cover that carries a data plate, numbered sections on thumb tabs, limitations, emergency and normal procedures as challenge-and-response checklists, and performance figures that state their conditions. The light theme is the printed page, ink on paper. The dark theme is the instrument face, light markings on a dark ground.

The surface is github.com, so the system has exactly two layers. GitHub owns everything Markdown renders: the body face and its sizes, heading sizes and their rules, table borders and striping, code-block grounds and their monospace face, link colour, alert colours and icons, the column width and every breakpoint. None of those are tokens here and none may be overridden. The project owns the SVG figures in `docs/assets/`, drawn by `docs/assets/src/build.py`, and the conventions for how Markdown is written. Every token in this file is a value in that script; the figures' two palettes are GitHub's own light and dark page colours, so a figure sits on the page as if printed on it.

Lettering in figures is Helvetica and Courier converted to outlines, because an SVG shown through an image tag cannot load fonts. One image file serves desktop and phone, so lettering is sized for the phone: the cover is shown at about 0.41 of its drawn size there and a figure at 0.6, which puts the smallest lettering at about 10 px.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Ink and paper, plus an instrument-marking code (green, yellow, red) that appears only where it encodes a measurement or a limit.
- Two faces with fixed jobs: Helvetica Bold capitals name things, Courier states data.
- Square, flat, ruled. No shadows, no gradients, no rounded containers.
- Every figure exists as a light and a dark file, chosen by the reader's colour scheme.
- Checklists set in 38 monospace columns with dot leaders, so they fit a phone without wrapping.
- Static. The one moving element is the take-off sequence, a recording.

### Launcher

**Launcher North Star: "The Launcher, Played Straight"**

The launcher is a modern game launcher and nothing else: one calm dark window, a panel with a backdrop, one bright action and one line of status under the title. It keeps the company its owner named, the RSI Launcher and CurseForge, and it refuses two things: the costume of an instrument panel, and anything a person has to read before they can press the button. A first launcher built in the handbook's style was rejected by the owner on 12 November 2026; this system replaces it for the app and leaves the README as it was.

The surface is a web page in a system web view inside a macOS window on Apple silicon: 1180 by 760 by default, never smaller than 980 by 680, dark only, with the window's title bar transparent over the page. Every token of the launcher is a value in `launcher/ui/app.css`. The named values are custom properties on `:root`; the remaining fills are written in place in that stylesheet and are named here.

It is an app someone operates, so its materials are the ordinary ones of a desktop app: the system typeface, real controls with hover, focus, pressed and disabled states, and state colours that say ready, your turn and failed. It runs beside a game that needs the whole machine, so in flight the window holds still.

**Key Characteristics:**
- A neutral charcoal ground with three tonal layers above it, hairline borders and no drop shadows.
- One blue for the action and for progress. Green, amber and red only for state, always beside words.
- The system typeface in sentence case: 12, 13 and 15 for the interface, then 20, 22 and 44, each with one job.
- Rounded: 10 for controls, 12 for cards and the large buttons, 14 for the hero panel.
- Opaque fills on everything laid over the backdrop. No blur, no glass.
- A photograph for a backdrop: one of three from NASA's open archives, or the person's own picture. Nothing drawn or generated, and no imagery or logos of the game.
- Authored line icons of one weight.
- In flight nothing animates and nothing transitions.

## Colors

Two palettes of the same eight roles, one per GitHub theme; each figure is built once from each.

### Primary
- **Ink** (`ink` on the page, `ink-dark` on the instrument face): all lettering, frames, the solid fill of tabs, placards, kit-part labels and measured bars, axes and dimension lines.
- **Paper** (`paper`, `paper-dark`): the ground, equal to GitHub's page colour in that theme. Also the lettering colour on any ink-filled shape, and the knock-out patch laid behind lettering that a line would otherwise cross.

### Secondary
- **Limit Red** (`red`, `red-dark`): the limit. On the cover it is the span dimension labelled MEMORY 24 GB, AS TESTED on a second line; in the loading graph it is the vertical red line at 24 GB and its label, which names the test machine because the line sits at whatever memory a Mac has installed. Line weight 2.6 in the graph.
- **Emergency Red** (`tabred`, `tabred-dark`): the fill of the Section 3 thumb tab and of the hatched rule under that heading, nowhere else. In the light theme it is the same value as Limit Red; in the dark theme it is deeper, because it carries a white numeral (`emergency-numeral`) where Limit Red is drawn as thin lines and lettering on the dark ground.

### Tertiary
- **Normal-range Green** (`green`, `green-dark`): the part of a scale at or above the target, its label, and the dashed target line (1.5 wide, dashes 3 3) drawn behind each bar.
- **Caution Yellow** (`yellow`, `yellow-dark`): the part of a scale below the target. A band colour. In the light theme it reaches only about 3.1:1 against paper, so it is not a lettering colour there.

### Neutral
- **Muted** (`muted`, `muted-dark`): secondary lettering inside figures (notes beside a title, scale numerals, axis titles, legend text, the issue line on the cover, the as-found row) and the dot leaders.
- **Hairline** (`hair`, `hair-dark`): scale ticks behind bars and the rule that separates the as-found row, 1.5 wide.

### Named Rules
**The Marking Code Rule.** Green, yellow and red are instrument markings, not accents. A colour may appear in a figure only where it encodes a measured range, a target or a limit, and the figure must say in words what it means (GREEN: 25 AND ABOVE, RED LINE: TEST MACHINE, 24 GB). Everything else is ink on paper.

**The Two Reds Rule.** Limit Red marks a number the machine cannot exceed. Emergency Red marks Section 3 and only Section 3: its tab and the hatched rule under its heading. Neither is used for emphasis.

**The One Tint Rule.** Ink at 0.38 opacity has a single meaning: the measured spread at the end of a bar, where the true value lies somewhere inside. A dashed ink outline with no fill means not in memory or not part of the kit's result (data in swap, the as-found row). No other tints or fills exist.

### Launcher

One dark palette. The app has no light theme. Charcoal layers carry everything, one blue acts, and three state colours report.

**Primary**
- **Action Blue** (`launcher-accent`): the fill of the primary button, the bar's fill, the ring and centre of a running step, the busy dot, the switch when on, the text selection. `launcher-accent-up` is its lighter step, for the primary button under the pointer and for the focus ring. `launcher-on-accent` is the near-black lettering and icon on the blue, 6.8 to 1 against it.

**State**
- **State Green** (`launcher-ok`): the dot when ready and in flight, the check in a done step, a frame rate at or above the target.
- **State Amber** (`launcher-warn`): the dot, ring, state word and notice title when it is the person's turn, a frame rate below the target, and the part of the bar past this Mac's installed memory.
- **State Red** (`launcher-bad`): the dot, mark, state word and notice title of a failure or a stopped game, and the fill of the armed Stop button, with `launcher-on-bad` lettering.
- **State grounds**: `launcher-ok-ground` and `launcher-bad-ground` fill the round mark of a done and a failed step. `launcher-notice-warn` with its ring `launcher-notice-warn-line`, and `launcher-notice-bad` with `launcher-notice-bad-line`, are the two notices. Each is the state colour taken down to a dark, opaque ground.

**Neutral**
- **Ground** (`launcher-bg`): the window behind every page.
- **Rail** (`launcher-rail`): the sidebar.
- **Surface** (`launcher-surface`): tiles and the body of the segmented control.
- **Raised** (`launcher-raised`): the chosen segment and the count in the sidebar.
- **Hero ground** (`launcher-hero-ground`): the hero panel behind its backdrop. **Pane** (`launcher-pane`): the setup pane docked in the hero.
- **Control** (`launcher-control`, under the pointer `launcher-control-hover`): small buttons, the Stop button, the disabled primary button, the Soon tag. **Ghost fill** (`launcher-ghost`): the unavailable Multiplayer button.
- **Bar track** (`launcher-track`) and **Bar cut** (`launcher-cut`): the empty bar and the 2 px cuts that divide it.
- **Hairline** (`launcher-line`) and **Strong hairline** (`launcher-line-strong`): white at 8 and 16 per cent. The first is every border and row divider. The second is the inset ring of a control or an empty step mark.
- **Tints** (`launcher-hover`, `launcher-selected`, `launcher-switch-off`): white at 5, 9 and 14 per cent, for a sidebar item under the pointer, the selected sidebar item and the switch when off.
- **Text** (`launcher-text`), **Muted** (`launcher-muted`), **Faint** (`launcher-faint`): three steps of lettering. Text is 17.7 to 1 on the ground, Muted 8.3 to 1, Faint 5.0 to 1 on the rail. Muted is for descriptions, notes and items not chosen. Faint is only for the sidebar's foot, the line under the tiles, the time stamps in the log and the idle dot.

### Launcher Named Rules
**The One Accent Rule.** One blue (`launcher-accent`) marks the action a person can take now and the progress of what is under way: the primary button, the bar's fill, the running step, the busy dot, the switch that is on, and in its lighter step the focus ring. There is no second accent, and blue never says that something is good or bad.

**The State Colour Rule.** Green, amber and red only say what state something is in. Green: ready, done, in flight, a frame rate at or above the target. Amber: the person's turn, a frame rate below the target, memory past what this Mac has installed. Red: a failure, the game stopped, the armed Stop. Each stands beside words that say the same thing, and none is decoration.

**The Solid Fill Rule.** Nothing of the backdrop shows through a control, a box or a pane. Everything laid on the hero has an opaque fill: the buttons, the tag, the bar's track, the setup pane, both notices. The title and the status line stand on the backdrop itself, behind a veil that darkens it from the left and from below. No blur and no glass. The white tints (`launcher-hover`, `launcher-selected`, `launcher-switch-off`) are used only over the rail and the ground, never over the picture.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Helvetica Bold, drawn as outlines (no fallback: there is no live text in a figure)
**Body Font:** Helvetica Regular, drawn as outlines, inside figures. README prose is GitHub's own sans-serif at GitHub's sizes.
**Label/Mono Font:** Courier and Courier Bold, drawn as outlines, inside figures. Checklists and commands are GitHub's own monospace.

**Character:** A printed technical manual. Helvetica Bold capitals with open tracking name a thing; Courier, the typewriter face of a data plate, states what was measured.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (`cover-title`: bold, 74, tracking -0.02 em): the name sc-offline-mac on the cover, lower case, once.
- **Headline** (`cover-designation`: bold capitals, 25, tracking 0.12 em; `placard`: bold capitals, 25, tracking 0.08 em): OPERATING HANDBOOK under the title, and the boxed placard.
- **Title** (`figure-title`: bold capitals, 17, tracking 0.05 em): names inside figures: layer names, row titles, CONDITIONS, axis titles.
- **Body** (`cover-description`: regular, 27, lines 34 apart; `figure-text`: regular, 17): sentences and notes inside the cover and figures.
- **Label** (`cover-data-label` and `cover-data-value`: Courier Bold and Courier, 24, rows 37 apart; `figure-data` and `figure-data-quiet`: Courier Bold and Courier, 17): data. Bold for the label of a plate row and for a value that is the point of the figure; regular for the value beside a label, conditions, scale numerals.
- **Tab numeral** (`tab-numeral`: bold, 20 in a 32 square): the section number.

### Named Rules
**The One Size Rule.** Inside a figure every piece of lettering is 17. Hierarchy comes from face, weight, capitals and ink against muted, never from a second size. The cover is the only plate with a scale, and its smallest lettering is 24.

**The Phone Floor Rule.** Lettering is sized by what it becomes in a 360 px column: drawn size times 0.6 for a figure, times 0.41 for the cover, and the result stays at about 10 px. A new figure keeps the 600 measure and the 17 lettering rather than adding detail that needs smaller type.

**The Outline Rule.** Every letter in a figure is a path produced by `textpath` from Helvetica or Courier. No `<text>` elements, no font references, so each figure carries its full reading in the `aria-label` and `<title>` and again in the `alt` of the image.

**The Written Range Rule.** A measured range is written with the word "to" (16 to 25 FPS), in prose, tables and figures alike. An en dash in a table cell means not measured.

### Launcher

**Display, body and label font:** the system face of the Mac (`-apple-system`, which is SF Pro, then BlinkMacSystemFont, "SF Pro Text", system-ui, "Helvetica Neue", sans-serif). One family for everything.
**Mono font:** ui-monospace ("SF Mono", then Menlo), on the Log page only.

**Character:** The typeface of the machine the app runs on, in sentence case. It is the native material of a Mac app, and it is used plainly: weight and colour make the hierarchy, not capitals, tracking or a second family.

**Hierarchy**
- **Display** (`launcher-title`: 700, 44, line height 1.04, tracking -0.03 em): the game's name on the hero, once. It is 38 in a window 1080 wide or less (`launcher-title-narrow`) and 36 in a window 720 high or less (`launcher-title-short`).
- **Headline** (`launcher-page-title`: 650, 22, tracking -0.015 em): the title of the Setup, Settings and Log pages.
- **Title** (`launcher-figure`: 650, 20, tracking -0.015 em, tabular numerals): the value in a tile.
- **Lead** (`launcher-lead`: 600, 15; `launcher-tagline`: 400, 15; `launcher-brand`: 650, 15, tracking -0.01 em): the status line, the large buttons and a row's heading; the line under the title; the name in the sidebar.
- **Body** (`launcher-body`: 400, 13, line height 1.45): the default. Descriptions keep a measure of 58 to 78 characters.
- **Label** (`launcher-label`: 500, 13; `launcher-label-strong`: 600, 13): sidebar items and segments; small buttons, the notice title, the pane's title.
- **Small** (`launcher-small`: 400, 12; `launcher-small-strong`: 600, 12): notes and states; the Soon tag, the count, the current stage under the bar.
- **Mono** (`launcher-log`: 400, 12, line height 1.5): the lines of the log.

### Launcher Named Rules
**The Three Small Steps Rule.** All running interface text is 12, 13 or 15. 13 is the default, 12 is for notes and states, 15 is for a line that leads: the status line, a large button, a row's heading, the tagline. Above them are three sizes with one job each: 20 for a tile's value, 22 for a page title, 44 for the game's name. Inside a step, hierarchy comes from weight (400, 500, 600, 650, 700) and from text against muted against faint.

**The Sentence Case Rule.** Everything is in sentence case at normal tracking. Capitals are not a style here and no label is tracked out. Negative tracking belongs only to the four largest settings: the brand at 15, the tile value at 20, the page title at 22 and the title at 44.

**The Tabular Numerals Rule.** Any number that can change while a person watches is set in tabular numerals, so the line does not shift: the figure at the right of the status line, the note under the bar, tile values and notes, step states, the count in the sidebar, the version.

## Layout

The column, its width and its breakpoints belong to GitHub. Two measures are set by the project.

- **The cover measure (880).** The cover is the title page and takes the full column, left-aligned in a plain paragraph, 880 by 700, inside a 2-wide ink frame with a 40 margin. The hatched rule shares this measure.
- **The figure measure (600).** Every other figure and every photograph or recording is 600 wide, centred, with its caption centred beneath it after a line break. Drawn figures keep a 20 margin inside the measure; scales run from 20 to 580.

Order of a page: the cover, one opening paragraph that links to Section 4, the Notice, Performance specifications, the take-off sequence, the table of sections, then the nine sections. The three front-matter headings (Notice, Performance specifications, Sections) carry no tab; each of the nine section headings begins with its thumb tab.

Within a figure: boxes are inset 18 from their frame, stacked boxes are 10 apart, a kit-part row is 38 high, a frame-rate row is 52, a loading-graph row is 96. A dot leader starts 12 after its label.

Tables are GitHub's. Columns are left-aligned; only the section-number column is centred. A performance table never stands alone: a **Conditions.** paragraph (profile, resolution, sync, place, runs, date, how it was read) comes before it, or a line stating the runs and date follows it directly.

Checklists are fenced blocks with no language, every line at most 38 characters and every challenge-and-response line exactly 38, which fits a phone column without horizontal scrolling. Commands go in a separate `bash` block after the checklist, never inside it.

### Launcher

A window, not a page. The body is a grid of two columns that fills the window and does not scroll: the sidebar (208) and the page.

- **Sidebar (208).** Inset 46 from the top, which clears the window's title bar controls, 12 at the sides, 16 at the bottom. From the top: the name, the four pages (Play, Setup, Settings, Log) as items 36 high and 2 apart, and at the foot the two standing rules and the version.

When there is only a message it shrinks to the notice alone, at the top right, with no ground of its own. Otherwise it is absent and the backdrop has the whole panel.

Setup's rows are at most 900 wide and 14 above and below. Settings' rows are at most 860 wide and 16 above and below, label at the left, control at the right.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat. There are no shadows, no gradients and no blurred edges anywhere in the figures, and Markdown offers none. Order is shown the way a drawing shows it: a paper-coloured patch is laid behind lettering so that a line passes behind it (the red line behind row titles in the loading graph, the scale behind value labels), and the open lid in the cover's side view is an ink stroke with a paper stroke down its middle.

### Launcher

Flat, by tonal layering. Depth is four charcoal steps (ground, rail, surface, raised) and hairlines. There is no drop shadow anywhere in the app. The only `box-shadow` values are inset rings that act as borders: 1 px of a hairline on a control or a notice, 1.5 px on a step mark.

Gradients exist in two places and nowhere else: in the veil over the backdrop (over a photograph that comes with the app: from below, 82 per cent of near-black falling to 50 at a third of the height and to none at 62 per cent, and from the left, 42 per cent falling to none at 58 per cent of the width; over a picture of the person's own, which may be anything, the heavier one: from the left, 88 per cent falling to none at 76 per cent of the width, and from below, 55 per cent falling to none at 46 per cent of the height), and in the bar's fill, where blue meets amber at a hard stop with no blend. No blur is applied to any part of the interface.

## Shapes

Square and ruled. Frames, tabs, placards, kit-part labels, bars and bands have square corners (radius 0). Outer frames and layer boxes are 2 wide in ink; axes are 2; hairlines, ticks and leaders are 1.5; dimension lines are 1.2 to 1.8.

Rounded corners exist only where a drawing depicts a rounded object: the Mac's lid, screen, notch and base on the cover. They describe the machine, not the system.

Line patterns each mean one thing:
- **Dot leader** (1.5 wide, round caps, dash 0.01 5, muted): joins a label to its value.
- **Short dash 3 3, green:** the target.
- **Dashed outline, ink** (3 2.4 at bar scale, 5 3.5 at load-bar scale): a quantity that is not in memory or not the kit's result.
- **Hatch** (7 of red in every 14, at 45 degrees, 14 high): the emergency edge.

Arrowheads are solid triangles, a little over a third as wide as they are long, at the ends of dimension lines.

### Launcher

Rounded, in a short scale where each step has its owners:

- **3** (`launcher-bar`): the bar.
- **8** (`launcher-tag`): the Soon tag and a segment inside the segmented control.

### Launcher Named Rules
**The Said Once Rule.** The status line says the state once: a dot, one sentence, one figure at its right. Nothing else on the page repeats that sentence. The command a person needs is on the primary button and is not repeated in the notice beside it.

**The Source On This Mac Rule.** No number without a source on this Mac. Every figure the launcher shows is read from the machine it runs on, or says where it was measured. A reading that does not exist yet is an en dash, never an estimate. Opened without the app, the page says "Simulated readings." in front of the line that names the one Mac measured, and the version reads "simulator".

**The Own Picture Rule.** The launcher carries no imagery, logo or interface styling of the game. The backdrop is a photograph: one of three from NASA's open archives that come with the app, each credited on the panel, or a picture the person chose, which stays on their Mac. Nothing on it is drawn or generated.

**The Corrected In Place Rule.** When the engine reports, a row, a tile or a control is corrected where it stands. Nothing is rebuilt and nothing moves without cause; the row that needs the person is scrolled into view.

**The Reveal Rule.** The bar's fill always spans the whole track and is revealed with a clip-path over 0.5 s. It is never resized, so its two colours stay where they belong on the scale.

**The At Rest In Flight Rule.** While the game runs, nothing animates and nothing transitions: every transition and animation in the window is switched off for the flight phase, because the game needs the machine. Readings still change; they change in place.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** draw every new figure in `docs/assets/src/build.py` from the `THEMES` roles, and ship it as a `-light` and a `-dark` file placed with `<picture>`.
- **Do** keep figures at the 600 measure with all lettering at 17, and the cover alone at 880.
- **Do** use Helvetica Bold capitals for names and Courier for data, as outlines, and give each figure an `aria-label` and an `alt` that state every value it shows.
- **Do** say in words what a marking colour means inside the figure that uses it.
- **Do** hold checklists to 38 columns with full-stop leaders and capital responses, and put commands in their own `bash` block.
- **Do** state conditions with every performance table, and sign each photograph or recording with its run and date.
- **Do** begin each of the nine section headings with its thumb tab, shown at 28 by 28.
- **Do** leave prose, tables, code blocks, links and alerts in GitHub's own styling.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use green, yellow or red for emphasis, decoration or status. They are data; see The Marking Code Rule.
- **Don't** use the hatched rule or Emergency Red outside Section 3.
- **Don't** add shadows, gradients, tints or rounded containers. Rounded corners belong only to a drawing of a rounded object.
- **Don't** put live text or font references in an SVG, or add a second lettering size to a figure.
- **Don't** set lettering in light-theme Caution Yellow; it is a band colour.
- **Don't** add badges, logos, emoji bullets or a screenshot carousel, and do not use the game's logos, trade dress or interface styling.
- **Don't** invent approvals, stamps, serial numbers or signatures. The handbook borrows the form of an aircraft manual, not its authority.
- **Don't** add a fourth kind of notice or use an alert for anything but its defined meaning.

### Launcher, do:
- **Do** take every launcher colour from the `launcher-` tokens, and give a new surface a named value in `launcher/ui/app.css` before using it.
- **Do** keep one bright button on the Play page: `launcher-accent` with `launcher-on-accent` lettering, 52 high, radius 12.
- **Do** give every control its hover, focus, pressed and disabled state, and keep the focus ring: 2 px of `launcher-accent-up`, 3 px off the control.
- **Do** pair every state colour with words: a dot with its sentence, a coloured value with its note.
- **Do** set changing numbers in tabular numerals, and show an en dash where there is no reading yet.
- **Do** use the one easing, cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1), for a change of state, at 0.15 to 0.18 s, and transition only colour, transform, opacity and clip-path.
- **Do** draw a new icon on the 20 grid with a 1.6 round stroke in the current text colour.
- **Do** write ranges with the word "to" here as in the README, and label any reading that is simulated.

### Launcher, don't:
- **Don't** carry the handbook into the launcher: no Helvetica and Courier lettering, no tracked capitals, no thumb tabs, placards, dot leaders or hatching, no square ruled frames.
- **Don't** use green, amber or red for emphasis or decoration, and don't use blue to mean good.
- **Don't** add a second accent or a second bright button.
